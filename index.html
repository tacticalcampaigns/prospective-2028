How to read this: “2028 incumbent” is the winner of the most recent election for the seat — the 2026 winner for House and Congress, the 2024 winner for Senate (those seats were not on the 2026 ballot). Presumed incumbent: exactly one 2028 race is still uncalled as of the Nov 12 snapshot — HD48, where Jake Hunsaker (R) leads by 298 votes and the trailer would need 62% of the roughly 1,206 remaining district ballots. That seat carries a PRESUMED badge; every other 2028 incumbent is the decided winner of the most recent election for the seat. “Registered (active)” is active registered voters for 2026 only, from the Tableau voter-file extract (Ali Majid’s November 2026 Dashboard, published Nov 2, 2026); district-level registration for 2022 and 2024 is not publicly available, so those years show n/a — never an estimate. R / D votes are votes for Republican and Democratic candidates in that year’s general election, with each party’s share of all votes cast in the race in parentheses; votes for other parties and unaffiliated candidates are counted in Other votes and in the total. GOP strength 2024 is the generic Republican ballot share in 2024: (Republican votes for the four state constitutional offices in the 2024 general election — Governor, Attorney General, Treasurer, Auditor — divided by 4) ÷ total 2024 presidential votes in the district, computed from the official state 2024 results at precinct level and mapped to the current district lines. Change vs 2024 (pts) is that year’s GOP share of total votes minus the 2024 baseline, in percentage points — green with a + when the GOP share rose, red with a − when it fell — shown only for actually contested races (a dash when there is no R candidate, when Democrats take 100%, or when the R candidate takes 100%, and for years the seat was not up). Congressional districts, 2022/2024: those elections ran under the pre-2026 district lines and are not comparable to the current 2026–2032 court-ordered map, so no 2022/2024 congressional results are shown; the map renders those districts gray for those years. 2026CD: the 2026 U.S. House vote within each legislative district’s boundaries, aggregated from county precinct reports, shown as a baseline for comparison with the district’s own races. The leading party in a district is the party that received the most congressional votes within that district — not a candidate result. Congressional districts show their own 2026 race as the baseline. Coverage is partial until every county has published its precinct report; the coverage note shown in the 2026CD view lists which counties are in and how many precincts are counted. Sources: 2022 official precinct-level results; 2024 official state results export; 2026 state All-Results report as of the Nov 12, 2026 snapshot (state lastUpdated 2026-11-13T01:10:43Z).
